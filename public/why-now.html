The argument

Every jump in model capability makes this layer more necessary.

The reasonable objection to everything on this site is that the models will simply absorb it. Wait eighteen months, the argument goes, and the frontier will handle the messy parts that need a platform today. It is worth taking seriously, and we think it points the other way. Capability does not grant permissions, and it does not know your exceptions.

They know the world What a frontier model knows is what is written down publicly. How your company decides is not written down publicly, and it is not going to be.
Better models raise the stakes Each jump raises what a company is willing to hand over, and every one of those handovers needs grounding, permissions and something to be measured against.
The mix is the future Frontier, open weight and tuned models together, chosen per task. Every mix needs a layer that does not belong to any one provider.

The thing models cannot get

Which fields mean what. Which exceptions matter. Who decides.

A model is trained on the public record. Your company's operating knowledge is the opposite of public: it lives in the heads of people who have been doing the work for nine years, in the exceptions nobody documented, in the reason a rule that looks arbitrary exists. None of that reached the training set of any model, and no amount of scale changes that, because the material was never available to scale over.

So as general capability improves and becomes cheaper, the part that stays scarce is the part only you have. That is a strange kind of good news. The advantage moves toward the companies whose AI learned their specific business, and away from the ones renting general intelligence and hoping it guesses right.

Everyone is attempting this, and most attempts are failing the same way.

The failure is consistent enough to have been measured by people with no stake in our argument. It is worth reading these as a description of a missing layer rather than as a verdict on AI.

What that looks like from inside

Four things fail at once, and they are one missing environment.

These arrive as four separate complaints from four different parts of a company, which is exactly why they get funded as four separate projects and solved by none of them.

01

The people who know the work are blocked

Ops, sales, finance and support were handed AI tools and hit a wall. The tools know nothing about the company, cannot reach real data safely, and have nowhere governed to deploy. Every request routes back to engineering, and whatever does work stays on one person's laptop.

02

The programme stalls before it reaches the numbers

Pilots demo well and die before they touch a profit and loss statement. Nobody budgeted for the integration and learning layer, so that is reliably where the money runs out.

03

Cost compounds in the wrong direction

Everything runs through frontier tokens with no accounting per outcome and no route to a cheaper specialist model, because nobody owns the material you would need to build one.

04

What you learn flows outward

Prompts, traces and especially corrections carry how the company operates, and they move one way, toward whichever vendor is holding them. You end up paying for intelligence twice: once in cash, and once in what you had to reveal to make it useful.

What gets sold as the cure is a multi-year migration: replace the legacy systems, move the data, rebuild the pipelines, and at the far end of all that, AI. Most buyers we meet have already sat through one of those and watched it stall. The destination is not in doubt. What none of them has is somewhere to do the work.

The objection, answered directly

Suppose the models get much better. Three things happen, and none of them is this layer disappearing.

More surface

Capability raises what gets handed over

Better models mean more agents doing more consequential work, and every one of them still needs to know how this company operates, what it may touch, and what it will be measured against. A more capable model asking for a wider permission is a governance problem arriving sooner.

The test

Take any one model away

If the company keeps its capability when a provider is removed, the capability was never inside the model. The brain, the rules, the corrections and the traces stay where they are, and the agents keep running on whatever you point them at next.

nothing of yours was ever inside them

Where this is heading

Not one model. A mix, chosen per task, changing every few months.

Enterprises are already asking for provider neutral architecture, looking hard at open weight models for cost and sovereignty, and starting to ask whether they could produce training material of their own rather than renting intelligence indefinitely. Each of those pushes the same way.

Frontier

The hard reasoning, the novel cases, the work where being right matters more than what it costs.

Open weight

Self hosted where data cannot leave, or where a per token bill would make the workflow unaffordable.

Tuned

Trained on how this company does one function, and better than a general model at exactly that one thing.

Distilled

Small models carrying the routine majority of the work at a fraction of the cost of pushing it through the frontier.

every mix needs the same two things

a layer that belongs to none of them · your own material as fuel

Meanwhile every layer a company already owns is selling it AI: the clouds, the SaaS vendors, the labs. Each of those exists to defend its own platform, which is a perfectly rational thing for them to do and also the reason none of them can be the neutral layer that spans all of it. That part has to come from someone independent.

What follows for the decision in front of you

The one thing that cannot be bought later is the record of how you decide.

Almost everything in this market can be adopted late. Models improve while you wait, tooling gets cheaper, and the company that moves in eighteen months buys a better version of what you would buy today. The exception is capture. Six months of traced runs and reviewed corrections is not something a budget can produce in a quarter, because it is made of work your people have already done.

That is the honest reason to start now rather than an urgency we invented. The first phase is worth doing on its own terms, and it happens to be the only way to have the material the later phases need.
