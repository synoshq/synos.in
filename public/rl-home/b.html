The hard work happens before the first training run.

GPUs and training recipes are easy to rent. Six things upstream of them are where most attempts stall.

The challengeWhat it costs a team today
The environmentAgents need somewhere to act on real systems without consequences. Teams build copies and sandboxes, which drift from the business. What an agent learns there stops holding once it meets production.
The dataHistorical records are scattered across systems, inconsistent and unlabelled. What experts know lives in their heads. Engineers spend the opening stretch of a project reconciling records by hand, and the expert knowledge never reaches a dataset.
The rewardSomeone has to decide what correct means, per task, and keep it current. Without a scored definition of correct, nobody can say whether a new model is better than the one it replaces.
The teamResearch engineers who build environments and run post-training are scarce and costly. Most companies cannot hire them, so the project waits on a hire that may never close.
The loopCorrections made in production rarely flow back into the model. Experts fix the same mistake again and again, and the model never sees the fix.
SovereigntyTraining data cannot leave the company's boundary. A service that trains outside your cloud can be ruled out at security review, along with the work already done to prepare for it.

SynOS is three parts, installed in your cloud.

Each one does a piece of the upstream work, so a small team of domain experts and engineers can run its own post-training lab.

The Brain

The context every agent and every training run reads from.

It connects the systems and historical data you already have and resolves the same entity across every source. It also holds the procedures your experts follow, written where an agent can read them.

The Agent Runtime

Where agents work on live data with nothing at stake.

Every write is intercepted and recorded rather than executed, then scored. Experts correct agents in the tools they already use, and every run is priced per model and per provider.

The Training Plane

Where runs and corrections become a model you own.

It turns them into evals and training data, improves agents with prompt optimisation (GEPA), then post-trains an open-weight model with SFT and RL with verifiable rewards. Platform agents build the environments and clean the data, and a release that scores worse than the last one does not ship.

How a correction becomes a model.

Your systems and data become context that every agent reads. Agents, apps and MCP servers run on it within your governance, and every run feeds the models you train, which go back into the model layer.

Questions a technical buyer asks first.

Do we need an ML or research team?

No. Platform agents build the training environments and clean the data, which is the work that usually needs research engineers. Your engineers connect the systems and your domain experts correct the agents.

Where do our data and the model live?

In your own cloud account. SynOS installs there, training runs there, and the weights it produces are stored there, so training data stays inside your boundary.

Which models does it use?

Post-training works on open-weight models. Agents can run on a hosted model while yours is trained, and every run is priced per model and per provider so you can compare the two on the same work.

What are GEPA, SFT and RL with verifiable rewards, in plain words?

GEPA is prompt optimisation: it rewrites an agent's instructions from its scored runs and your experts' corrections, before any weights change. SFT trains the model on worked examples of the right answer, and RL with verifiable rewards lets it attempt a task many times and rewards the attempts a check can confirm are correct.

What happens to the model and data if we stop?

They stay where they already are, in your cloud. The weights are open weights in your account, so you can keep serving the model, and the evals, training data and recorded runs remain yours.

How much expert time does it take?

Experts review and correct agent work in the tools they already use, as part of the job they do today. The corrections carry the most weight early on, and there are fewer of them to make as the model improves.

What does a first use case look like?

One workflow, the experts who own it and the systems it touches. Agents run on live data with their writes recorded, experts correct them, and those corrections become the first evals and the first training set.

How is this different from a hosted fine-tuning API?

A fine-tuning API expects a finished dataset and trains on its provider's infrastructure. SynOS builds that dataset from your systems and your experts' corrections, trains inside your cloud and gates each release on your own evals.
