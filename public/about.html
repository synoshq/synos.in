About

This problem needs two disciplines that rarely turn up together.

One half is data infrastructure: making messy operational data usable without moving it, binding what a metric means to the column it actually lives in, and keeping that true as everything underneath shifts. The other half is enterprise infrastructure software: the kind that installs inside someone else's datacentre, upgrades without a call, and has to be correct when nobody from your team is watching. Most attempts at this problem are strong in one half and learning the other on the customer's time.

Data infrastructure and semantic layers

Profiling systems in place, resolving entities across systems with no shared key, binding metrics with invariants that catch a rebind gone wrong. This is the piece that decides whether an answer is grounded or plausible, and it is where projects like this fail without anyone noticing for a quarter.

Software that ships into someone else's building

Air-gapped installs, upgrade paths, revocation, audit a reviewer will sign, and behaviour that holds up with no operator present. Enterprise buyers can tell within one conversation whether a team has done this before, because the questions they ask are unglamorous and specific.

How we work

We run the company on the product.

Our own go to market, research and operations run as agents on this layer, against our own systems, with the same governed tools and the same correction loop a customer gets. That is partly conviction and partly self defence: the fastest way to find out that something is annoying to operate is to have to operate it every day.

It also sets the standard for what we say publicly. Every claim on this site is either something running in production, something marked as in build, or something marked as roadmap, and those chips are checked against an internal capability audit rather than against what would read well. Where a control is built but not switched on, this site says so. A caveat you find yourself is worth less to us than one we gave you.
