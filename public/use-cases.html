Before anything is bought

Half of what an agent gets wrong is not worth buying a layer for.

Sort your failed runs into two piles first. One pile is fixed by a prompt and a cheap evaluation tool. The other is fixed by giving the agent things that were never written down, and nothing on the market reaches those from your logs.

Six things teams say on a first call

What the layer does about each one.

“It answers confidently, and it is wrong about that particular customer.”

Your systems, and each of your customers’ systems, curated into context an agent can act on

Connectors across the systems you already run, column level profiling, entity resolution across sources and the links between them, on live data with the rows staying where they are. The exceptions your best operator applies without thinking get captured as they correct real work, rather than in an interview that happens once.

“We left it read only, because nobody would sign off on it writing.”

Agents built on it rehearse against your real systems, and the write each one would have made is scored

Reads run against live data, so the state is genuinely this morning’s rather than a copy taken in March. The writes are intercepted and recorded instead of committed, then compared against what should have been written. That comparison carries a fifth of the score, which is the part most evaluation tools never build.

“Fine for one step. By step nine it has gone somewhere we cannot defend.”

The whole trajectory is scored, not the paragraph at the end

Which tools it reached for, in what order, and with what arguments. Order is scored separately because sequence matters when one step authorises the next: checking eligibility after issuing the credit is a different act from checking it before, and an output diff cannot tell them apart.

“It worked until we priced it.”

Every run priced, per model and per provider, so you can see where the money actually goes

Token and money accounting lands on every run across three providers, broken down by step. That is what tells you which steps genuinely need the frontier model and which are being paid for out of habit, and it is the measurement a smaller model has to be argued from.

“We changed one thing to fix a case and broke four others without noticing.”

Nothing is promoted until the suite has been scored with and without it

A regression gate re scores the whole suite both ways and blocks anything that comes out worse. Train and holdout are separated at the API boundary and the excluded count is returned rather than hidden, so nobody trains on their own benchmark by accident.

“Eventually we want a model of our own.”

It accumulates on your own infrastructure in the shape a training set is built from

Every correction and every scored trajectory lands in your estate, split into train and holdout at the point it is written. The hard part of a model of your own has never been the training run. It is arriving at one with a clean, labelled, domain specific corpus that nobody else could assemble, and that is what this is building for you from the first week.
