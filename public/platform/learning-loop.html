Platform/Learning loop

A correction made once should never have to be made again.

In most AI deployments the person who spots the mistake fixes that one answer, and the same mistake arrives again on Thursday. The knowledge that would have prevented it stays where it started, in the head of someone who has now explained it four times. This is the part of the platform that stops that, and it is also where the record accumulates that decides how much you eventually let an agent do on its own.

Corrections compound One fix becomes a rule the next run inherits, context in the brain, and a case the agent is measured on from then on.
Promotion is a score An agent runs with less supervision when it clears a threshold on cases your own team wrote. A regression sends it back down.
Capture cannot be backfilled Six months of real corrections is an asset you can only get by having recorded them, which is why it runs from your first week.

What one correction becomes

Someone fixes an answer on a Tuesday. Four things happen to it.

A correction arrives as an ordinary act of work: a support lead edits what an agent drafted, an ops manager says that is not how the escalation runs. Reviewed once, it fans out into every place it needs to reach, and none of that costs the person who made it another minute.

The loop, in three turns

Humans correct. Agents act. Your systems return what actually happened.

Each turn produces a record no public model has ever seen, because it only exists inside your work. Stored and never read, that record is a log. Fed back, it is the reason the same agent is better in March than it was in January without anyone rewriting it.

TURN 01

A person corrects

In chat, in Slack, in the tool they already work in. No form, no ticket, no separate labelling exercise bolted on to their week.

“T3 billing escalations never go by SMS. Email finance and copy the account owner.”

TURN 02

Agents act on it

Once reviewed and promoted, the rule reaches every agent through the brain, whichever harness or model they happen to be running on.

The next run routes to email without being told again.

TURN 03

Systems return the result

The decision trace holds the input state, the action and what came back, so the value of a change is measured rather than assumed.

Did the escalation actually resolve faster.

Review sits between the first turn and the second on purpose. A correction is a proposal until someone with the authority to set policy accepts it, which is what stops one strong opinion on a bad afternoon from becoming how the company operates.

What decides a promotion

An agent moves up when it can show it got better.

Public benchmarks cannot help here. The exceptions that make your business yours are exactly the thing a general eval has never heard of, so the set has to be written by the people who know the work and grown out of the work itself.

01

Your team writes the cases

Ten or twenty real ones, each with the answer you would have given. It takes an afternoon from the people who already know which cases are the hard ones, and it is the only part nobody can do for you.

02

Every change gets replayed

A new skill, an edited rule, a swapped model. All of it runs against the whole set before it reaches production, so you see the effect of a change in advance rather than discovering it in a customer thread.

03

The score gates the rung

Assisted, reviewed, supervised, autonomous. Each rung sits behind a threshold your team sets, and a drop blocks the promotion and names which cases moved.

04

Corrections become new cases

When someone fixes an answer in the flow of work, that becomes a case the agent is measured on from then on. The set grows out of real work instead of a spec exercise nobody has time for.

Where this actually is today

Tracing and correction capture run in production now. Every run and every fix is already recorded against the context pack that produced it, which is the part you cannot go back and recreate later.

The replay harness and threshold gating are in build. Scoring against measured business outcomes rather than against a written answer is the piece after that.

Earning autonomy

Autonomous never means unsupervised, and it is granted per function.

One function can be running on its own while another next to it is still drafting for a human to send. What moves a function up is evidence, and every rung keeps the same permissions, the same audit trail and the same kill switch as the one below it.

Better context and stronger evals are what let more functions climb. You never hand off what you cannot measure, which is the honest reason most agent pilots stay at level one forever.

Where this is going

The same record is what a model of your own would be trained on.

A decision trace holds the context an agent was given, the action it took, the correction a person made and the outcome your systems returned. That is the shape of a training example, which means the work of making agents useful this quarter is also the work of assembling something no competitor starting today can assemble. It accrues on your infrastructure, and it pays off whichever model turns out to win.

The other two halves

Learning needs something to learn about and somewhere safe to apply it.

Company Brain

Where a correction lands

Promoted rules, entities and curation decisions live in the brain, which is what every agent reads before it acts, whatever tool the person is sitting in.

Governance

What holds at every rung

Identity, scoped tools, audit on every call and an immediate kill switch apply the same way at level four as they do at level one.

Bring ten cases you already argue about.

The clearest way to see whether this holds is to hand it the exceptions your team knows a general model gets wrong, and watch what happens the second time.
