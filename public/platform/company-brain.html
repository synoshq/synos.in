Platform/Company Brain

Your systems get profiled where they sit, and the profile is kept honest.

An agent is only as correct as the slice of context it was handed. The Company Brain is what decides that slice: what your entities mean, where each fact actually lives, which source to trust when two disagree, and what your people have taught it since. It holds the meaning of your data, and the rows stay in the systems that own them.

Profiled, not copied Agents read your schemas, samples, documents and APIs, and build the maps. Statistics and column roles come to us; the rows are queried live at answer time.
Refreshed on a schedule A brain built once starts going stale the same week. Profiling re-runs, diffs itself against what it believed, and applies what it is confident about.
Unsure is a state it can be in Anything ambiguous stops and becomes a review item with a name on it, instead of a confident answer nobody can trace.

What actually moves

The rows never leave your systems.

A migration copies your tables into a vendor tenancy and the copy is what the AI reasons over, which is why the data programme has to finish before anything useful runs. Profiling inverts that. What SynOS holds is the statistics, the semantics and the bindings: which table is authoritative for revenue, what a status field means, which join reaches the customer. The answer is assembled by querying your systems at the moment the question is asked.

Getting the right slice

The long context window made retrieval more important, not less.

A model handed a million tokens of loosely related material answers worse and costs more than the same model handed the four things that mattered. Most answers that get blamed on the model are a retrieval problem. A router picks the mode per query, so an order ID goes somewhere different from a question about why an account churned.

Hybrid with rerankBM25 + vector + RRF

Runs lexical and semantic in parallel, fuses the two rankings, then a cross encoder reorders the top of the list for precision.

production default, prose mixed with literal identifiers

Agenticplan, retrieve, reason

Decomposes a hard question into sub questions, runs each, merges and reranks what came back. Slower, and much better on the hard ones.

multi part questions, long threads, follow ups

Graph traversalentity hops

Walks linked entities and their relationships. Traversal happens at query time, so there is no giant persisted graph to keep in sync.

multi hop reasoning across connected entities

Whatever the mode, what comes back is a typed context pack: scoped to the caller's role and project, ranked by authority and freshness, fitted to a token budget, and carrying citation IDs so an answer can be traced to the row it came from. Withholding individual rows inside the brain itself depends on your sources being labelled first, and that enforcement is in build rather than live.

The part that decays

Schemas drift and definitions change. Agents keep answering anyway.

This is the failure mode nobody demos, because it takes a quarter to appear. A column gains three new enum values, a team renames a field, two tables both start claiming revenue, and the agent carries on sounding exactly as confident as it did in week one. So profiling runs on a schedule and holds a confidence score and a freshness stamp on every mapping.

What it is and is not

Three things people expect it to be, and what it does instead.

Not a warehouse It holds no copy of your operational rows and it is not where you run analytics. It knows which warehouse to ask and how to ask it.
Not an index you load There is no bulk load step to run before it works and no re-load when your data changes. Profiling reads your systems and refreshes the maps.
Not static retrieval Its content changes from use. Corrections, promoted rules and re-profiling results all land back in it, which is what the learning loop is for.

The other two halves

Context makes an agent correct. It does not make it safe or make it improve.

Governance

One governed door

Reading is the safe half. Every write goes through a single gate that checks role, tool, parameters and tenant, then writes an audit row whether it allowed the call or denied it.

Corrections that stick
