Platform/Governance

Agents can act in your real systems without anyone opening a hole.

Reading data is the safe half of the job. The moment an agent writes to a CRM, a ledger or a live campaign, the questions change: who was it acting as, what was it allowed to touch, who approved it, and where is the row that proves any of that. SynOS answers those the same way for a person clicking a button, an AI tool, and an agent running on a schedule at three in the morning.

One door Every caller reaches your systems through the same gate. There is no side path for the tool someone installed last week.
Denials are logged too An audit row is written whether the call was allowed or refused, so an attempt that should never have happened is visible rather than absent.
No keys on laptops Credentials stay on the server side of the gate. What an agent holds is a scoped token for the tools it was granted, and nothing else.

The path of one call

Follow a single write from the tool that asked for it.

The checks below run in order, and the first one that fails ends the call. What reaches your system is a request that carries an identity, a scope it was granted in advance, and parameters that matched a declared contract. What reaches your audit log is every attempt, in both directions.

Most of this exists in your stack already for human users. The gap that stops AI programmes is that none of it was built for a caller that is not a person, has no session, and can make four hundred calls in a minute.

The rails are set once. What gets authored on top cannot widen them.

Governance that depends on every author making good choices fails at the tenth author. Here the boundary is structural: an ops lead writing a skill in plain English is composing tools that engineering already declared, so there is nothing they can reach that was not granted in advance.

Engineering, set once

  • Connectors and data scopes: what any agent may read
  • Governed tools and write actions: what may be done, and what needs an approval
  • Roles, tenancy, audit retention, sandbox policy
  • Cost attribution per agent and per project, and the ceilings that go with it
  • Promotion thresholds: the score an agent must hold to run with less supervision

Reviewed once as infrastructure, rather than re-argued for every new request that comes in.

The other blast radius

An internal app your ops team shipped is code running against your systems.

This is where the AI app builders leave you. A tool that lets a non engineer ship a working app in an afternoon has, by default, shipped it to a personal account with a secret in the client bundle and permission to call anywhere. Every build here goes through the same five stages.

Build

03

04

Sandbox

Deployed to an internal URL, running behind the egress proxy, inside your own environment.

05

Audit row

Who shipped what, when, with which scopes, and what the scan found on the way through.

Agents also need somewhere structured to write. Outputs land in project scoped collections with an enforced schema, audited on every read and every write, so the next run can read what the last one produced instead of it ending up in a spreadsheet nobody can find.

What your security review will ask

The questions that arrive in every questionnaire, answered here.

The full control set and our subprocessor position live on the trust pages. These are the five that decide whether a review continues.

Where does it run?

Inside your own environment. Your VPC, your Kubernetes, or an air gapped installation with no outbound path at all. There is no version of this where your operational data sits in our tenancy.

What leaves the building?

Whatever you send to the model provider you chose, and nothing else. Point it at a model in your own account, or at one running on your own hardware, and the answer becomes nothing.

Who can an agent act as?

A run a person started carries that person, and its reach is bounded by roles and by the tools that were declared in advance. Escalation is not a decision anything makes at runtime. For unattended runs the durable grant that names a subject and re-checks their authority per call is built and not yet enabled, so treat a scheduled agent today as running with the scope its project was given rather than with a named person's.

How do we stop it?

A kill switch at the level of one agent, one skill, or the whole installation, effective immediately and without a deploy. Sandboxed apps are revoked the same way.

What can we prove afterwards?

Every call, allowed or denied, with the caller, the tool, the parameters, the result and the context pack that informed it. An answer can be traced back to the rows it was built from.
