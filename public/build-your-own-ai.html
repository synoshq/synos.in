Phase two, on the same install

What it takes to make AI work here is what it takes to train AI on how you work.

Every serious attempt at AI of your own runs into the same wall, and it is never the model. It is that nobody has the material: examples of how this company actually decides, produced by the people who know, with a way to tell whether a change made things better. That material is a by-product of getting agents working in the first place, which is the only reason this is affordable. You are not funding a training programme. You are keeping what the first one produced.

Capture first Traces and corrections run from your first week, because capture is the one part of this that cannot be backfilled later.
Then a benchmark you own Cases mined from real runs and real corrections, scored against your outcomes rather than a public leaderboard.
Training is downstream Fine-tuning is one thing you can do with that material. The evaluation environment is worth having even if you never train anything.

The order is forced

Nothing new gets installed between one phase and the next.

The reason to care about the order is money. A company that treats phase two as a separate programme has to buy the capture, the governance and the environment twice, and the second purchase never gets approved. Doing the first phase properly is what earns the second one for free.

How an attempt is run safely

An agent rehearses against your real systems, and the write it would have made is caught.

This is the mechanism that makes the whole thing more than a slogan. An evaluation run reads from your live systems, so the state is genuinely today's state. Its writes are intercepted at the tool layer and recorded rather than committed, which means the thing being scored is the action the agent would actually have taken. Three modes, and you choose per suite.

Recorded replay

mock_all

Replays a recorded run with canned responses. Fast and free, and useful for catching an outright regression, though a candidate that leaves the recorded path is by definition no longer being tested against reality.

Real reads

real_reads

Real tools against real data with writes refused outright. What the agent sees is true; what it tries to do is stopped at the boundary rather than recorded.

Real reads, captured writes

live_reads_captured_writes

The same real execution, except each intended write persists to the result instead of happening. The refund that would have been issued, the record that would have been updated, the resource that would have been resized, all captured with their parameters and scored. Nothing reached your systems.

Write refusal during a training run is a single enforced seam rather than a convention, including a floor that refuses any write on execution paths where the capture intercept does not exist. That boundary has been tested by trying to break it, which is the only kind of test worth quoting.

What gets scored

The trajectory, including the write, rather than the paragraph at the end.

Evaluation tools score an output against an expected string. That works for a summariser and tells you almost nothing about an agent, which can reach a defensible answer through a sequence you would never have allowed. These are the default weights, and they are yours to change.

Around the scores sit two things worth naming. A regression gate re-runs a suite with and without a proposed change and blocks anything that scores worse, so a promotion is a measurement rather than an opinion. And the export that builds a training set returns only cases marked eligible, with the excluded holdout reported as a count, so contamination is prevented at the boundary rather than in a document telling people to be careful.

The two questions anyone with an ML background asks

Both are fair, and the honest answers are better than confident ones.

How many episodes will you actually have?

Far fewer than a lab, and we do not need lab volume, because the unit here is not a rollout. It is a decision an expert reviewed. A function in production generates hundreds of reviewed decisions a quarter, which is the right order for tuning a narrow task and for an evaluation suite that actually separates a good change from a bad one.

What nobody doing real enterprise work will ever have is the volume for training from scratch, because every episode is a real business action with a real cost. That is precisely why the vendors selling simulated environments simulate. The trade is that a few thousand of your examples are the genuine exceptions, and a million of theirs are invented.

What supplies the reward?

Today it is human. Approvals, rejections and corrections from the person who owns the process, captured while they do the work. That is a preference signal rather than a scalar reward, and it is what a small preference model learns from.

Tying a decision to what the business measured afterwards is the next layer, and we will say plainly that it is a causal inference problem rather than a plumbing one: delayed, confounded, and cleanly identifiable only where a business will tolerate a holdout. We would rather build the measured version slowly than publish a number that flatters us.

Stated plainly

Three things this is not, so nobody discovers them in month four.

Not a simulated environment There is no synthetic twin of your business here and no generated task set. The environment is your systems, which is the strength and also the reason volume stays modest.
Not a claim that corrections are labels A correction today is reviewed, promoted, and reaches every agent as a rule. Capturing corrections as trajectory level training pairs is work we have not finished.
Not a finished training stack Capture and rehearsal are live. Evaluation suites are in build. Fine-tuning and distillation on your own infrastructure are roadmap, and they are the last thing to arrive rather than the first.
