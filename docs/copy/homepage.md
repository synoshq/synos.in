## 4. Problem Section (v4 — punchline introduces "institutional brain")

**Section label**: The Problem

**Headline**:
## Intelligent agents are here. Tools are here. Your institutional brain isn't.

**Section sub**:
Agents can already take actions — draft, update, edit, send — across your systems. They just don't know your business well enough to do it reliably.

**Three cards (grid-3):**

### Card 1 — Today's AI tools are powerful. But they can't be productionized for teams.
Brilliant for individual productivity. Fragile for teams. No organizational memory. No RBAC. No safe, controlled tools for the systems that actually run your company. 89% of agent projects are stuck in pilots for exactly this reason.

### Card 2 — Your institutional knowledge is scattered across apps, docs, and humans.
Salesforce, BigQuery, Jira, Slack, Notion, critical spreadsheets, and the heads of a few key people. Custom ways of working, institutional rules, tribal knowledge — all fragmented. Nobody has the full picture, and no agent can draw from it.

### Card 3 — Agents don't understand how your company actually operates.
They can access your data, but they can't read your business. Your rules, your exceptions, your conventions, your judgment calls — none of it is in any system they can query. Without that context, agents guess. Confidently, and often wrongly.

## 5. The Insight (v4 — "institutional knowledge" as the moat)

**Section label**: The Insight

**Headline**:
## Models commoditize. Your institutional brain is the moat.

**Body** *(single column, centered, narrative)*:

A 1M-token context window still doesn't know which of your 50 data sources to check. Extended thinking doesn't help when the answer isn't in anything the model can access.

The gap between "impressive individual demo" and "runs critical workflows reliably for a team" isn't closed by better models. It's closed by giving every agent access to a structured, living layer of your company's institutional knowledge — the rules, exceptions, relationships, and judgment calls that define how you actually operate.

Your way of operating is the moat. Models will keep changing. Agent frameworks will come and go. **The institutional brain stays, accumulates, and compounds — regardless of which model or harness you use.** Model-agnostic. Framework-agnostic. Yours.

**This layer will always exist, no matter how smart the models get.** Build it once. Compound it forever.

## 8. Graduated Trust

**Section label**: Earned Trust, Not Assumed

**Headline**:
## Safety and control, from day one to autonomous action.

**Section sub**:
Agents start supervised and graduate only when they have earned trust — measured, not assumed. Per-tool permissions. Full audit trail. Your data, your control. No agent writes anything until you say it can.

**Three cards (keep existing trust-grid):**

### Supervised
Agent plans. Human approves every action. Zero risk. Validate accuracy in week one.

### Shadow
Agent executes reads. Write actions are logged, not applied. Build confidence without exposure.

### Autonomous
Full execution within guardrails. You review exceptions, not every action.

**Footnote line**:
Self-hosted by default. Your data never leaves your environment.
